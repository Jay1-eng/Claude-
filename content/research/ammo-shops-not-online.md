# Research brief — Ammo shops that still don't sell online

Researched 19 Oct 2026. The angle: why thousands of US gun and ammo shops still don't sell ammunition
on their own website, and whether that gap is a business opportunity. Every number below is tagged with
its source. Re-check anything marked **verify** before it goes on screen.

## Why there's no ready-made "list"
No public directory flags shops that lack a website. You can build one, though:
1. Download the ATF's monthly **FFL listing** (free, public; every licensee's business name and premises address).
2. Filter to **Type 01 dealers** and **Type 02 pawnbrokers**.
3. Look up each one in Google Places or Yelp and keep the ones with no website field, or with a site
   that has no cart. This step needs a Places API key, and the usual cost is a few cents per lookup.
Sample a single state or county first. Doing all 46,000 dealers at once is a lot of lookups.

**The gap in one line:** about 46,000 licensed dealers, but only a few hundred stores show up in online ammo
search. Most shops sell ammo only across the counter.

## Why most shops aren't online (the real reasons)
1. **Payment processors won't take them.** Square's terms ban ammunition sales. PayPal has barred online
   sales of guns and ammo since about 2003. Stripe and Shopify Payments are widely reported to decline
   firearms merchants (**verify** current policy text). Shops end up paying for "high-risk" processors.
2. **The state rules are a patchwork.**
   - California requires a point-of-sale background check on ammo (since July 2019) and bans
     direct-to-home delivery.
   - New York also requires point-of-sale checks, so online ammo has to ship to a licensed seller.
   - Connecticut, Illinois, Massachusetts and New Jersey require a buyer license or permit.
   - Hawaii and DC are effectively closed to online ammo.
   (Retailer guides and news sources; **verify** each state against official sources.)
3. **Shipping is hard.** Ammo ships as ORM-D / limited quantity: ground only, with hazmat paperwork, and it's
   heavy, so free shipping eats the ~25% margin fast.
4. **Merchant code 5723.** The new card code for gun and ammo stores is required in CA (from May 2025), CO and NY.
   About 17 states ban or limit its use (Kentucky's ban took effect July 2024). Some owners avoid
   anything that adds visibility. (**verify** the current count)
5. **The counter already works.** Range fees, gunsmithing, classes and **transfer fees** (a local shop
   charges roughly $25–$75 to receive a gun someone bought online; **verify**) pay the bills. Many owners
   don't see a reason to fight Palmetto State Armory or Lucky Gunner on price.

## What could change it
- **ATF proposed rule (docket ATF-2026-0266):** it would let dealers finish background-checked
  sales to same-state buyers *remotely*, using NIST IAL2/AAL2 identity verification. Comments closed
  6 Aug 2026; the rule is **not final**. Small dealers worry it helps big online sellers. It covers firearms,
  not ammo, but it pushes every shop toward needing an online checkout.
- **H.R. 7166, "Stop Online Ammunition Sales Act"** (introduced 20 Jan 2026) pushes the other way: in-person ID
  for every online ammo purchase. It has not been enacted.

## Video angles for the channel
- **Short, "The Catch":** "Gun stores barely make money on guns. Here's what actually pays the rent."
  The numbers: 8–14% on guns, ~25% on ammo, plus transfer and range fees.
- **Short, "Could You Start This?":** "The website business nobody wants: building online stores
  for gun shops." 46k dealers, banned by mainstream processors, so the niche agencies charge a premium. **verify** pricing.
- **Long-form, "Who Really Owns It":** "Why your local gun shop still doesn't have a cart" covers processors,
  state laws, shipping and the 2026 ATF rule.

## Channel risk (read before you schedule it)
- YouTube's firearms policy bans content that sells ammo or links to sellers. Keep it as **business
  analysis only**: no product links, no "where to buy", no affiliate links to ammo stores.
- Under the advertiser-friendly guidelines, firearms content often gets **limited ads**. Use B-roll of
  storefronts, receipts, card terminals and shipping boxes, not guns being fired. **verify** the current policy.
