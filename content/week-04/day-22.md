**Hook (first frame text):** "Your $5k month just became $0."

### Script (≈155 words)
| Time | Voice-over | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Pressure washing makes great money in May. Here's what kills it: November. | Frozen driveway + hook |
| 0:04 | In most of the US, the season is roughly March to October — about eight months. Then the phone stops, the water freezes, and so does your income. | "Season ≈ 8 months" |
| 0:12 | Here's the trap. A good month in season might clear five thousand dollars. New operators spend like that's every month. Then February arrives with four months of zero and a truck payment. That's why so many quit in year one — not a bad business, a bad calendar. | "$5k × 8 ≠ $5k × 12" |
| 0:25 | The fix is maths, not hustle. Take the season's total, divide by twelve, and pay yourself *that* every month. Eight months of five grand is forty thousand. That's thirty-three hundred a month, all year — not five. | "$40k ÷ 12 ≈ $3.3k/mo" |
| 0:36 | Then fill the gap. Operators who survive stack winter work: Christmas light installs, gutter cleaning, monthly contracts for restaurant dumpster pads and fleet washing that pay in January too. | "Winter stack: lights, gutters, contracts" |
| 0:46 | And one physical catch: a pump left with water in it cracks in the first hard freeze. Winterise it, or buy a new one in spring. | "Winterise the pump" |
| 0:51 | My take: seasonal businesses don't fail in winter. They fail in summer, when the owner forgets winter is coming. | |
| 0:56 | Tomorrow: septic pumping — four hundred dollars a visit, legally required. Follow. | End card |
