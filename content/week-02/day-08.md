### Script (≈155 words)
| Time | Voice-over | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Operators will tell you most new vending routes don't make it past the first year. Not because the machines break. Because of three things nobody mentions. | Dark machine, "Out of order" + hook |
| 0:08 | One: the location leaves. Your best machine is in an office. The office downsizes, moves, or goes remote. Your contract was never exclusive and never long. The asset is now a fridge in your garage. | Empty office, "Location ≠ yours" |
| 0:19 | Two: shrink. Machines get pried open. Cash gets skimmed. Chips expire. Operators commonly budget several percent of sales for theft and spoilage — on a machine clearing a hundred a month, that hurts. | Broken machine door |
| 0:28 | Three: your time. A route of twenty machines is a full day of driving every week, plus shopping at the warehouse club the night before. The "passive" business is a part-time delivery job. | Van, route map |
| 0:38 | And the trap that kills beginners fastest: the starter-package sellers. Brand-new machines at five to ten thousand dollars each, "placement included," in spots that do twenty bucks a week. The seller profits. You never do. | "$5–10k 'starter kit'" |
| 0:48 | My take: vending works for people who treat it like a logistics business and buy used. It fails for people who bought a dream. | |
| 0:53 | Tomorrow: parking lots — the most profitable dirt in the city. Follow. | End card |
